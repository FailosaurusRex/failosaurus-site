Privacy Policy

This site is run by one person, and the list of people who read it is, in a very real sense, a list of people who trusted me with an inbox. This page explains exactly what I collect, why, who else touches it, and how to get it deleted. I've tried to write it the way I'd want to read it.

Effective date: October 7, 2026 · Last updated: October 7, 2026

Short version

  • I collect your email address when you sign up for the newsletter, plus a few technical details needed to run the list (when you signed up, and two random tokens for confirming and unsubscribing).
  • I never sell, rent or share your data for marketing, and I don't use it to train AI.
  • No analytics, no ad trackers, no tracking cookies. Visitors don't get cookies from this site at all.
  • Two outside services see some data: my hosting and email provider (Hostinger) handles the database and the email delivery, and Google Fonts sees your IP address when a page loads, because the fonts are loaded from Google.
  • Sign-up uses double opt-in and every newsletter has a one-click unsubscribe that deletes your address right away.
  • You can ask for access, correction or deletion any time at hello@failosaurusrex.com. I'll answer within 30 days.

The short version is a summary, not a substitute. The full policy below is what applies.

Who is responsible

Failosaurus Rex ("we", "us", "our") is a personal brand and newsletter run by one individual based in Texas, United States, at failosaurusrex.com. That individual is the "controller" of your personal data in the sense used by laws like the GDPR: I decide what is collected and why.

There is no company, no data team and no ad network behind this site. If you have a privacy question, the person who answers is the person who runs the database. Contact details are in the Contact section.

This policy covers failosaurusrex.com, the newsletter and the emails we send, the public issue archive and RSS feed, and any email you send to hello@failosaurusrex.com.

That's the full list. We don't use your email address for advertising, profiling, or anything you wouldn't expect from a newsletter.

Email practices

Double opt-in

When you submit your address, we send one email containing a confirmation link. You are not added to the newsletter until you click it, so nobody can sign you up without access to your inbox. If you never confirm, you receive no newsletters. If the same address is submitted again before it has been confirmed, we generate a fresh link and send another confirmation email, and the older link stops working.

After you confirm, we send a short welcome note. After that, you'll only hear from us when there's a newsletter issue or an occasional service message.

How often

Roughly weekly. That's an intention, not a promise, so there may be gaps or the occasional extra note.

Unsubscribing

  • Every newsletter and the welcome email contain an unsubscribe link. Clicking it removes you straight away, with no login and no questions. Newsletters also carry a standard List-Unsubscribe one-click header, so Gmail, Apple Mail and others can show their own "Unsubscribe" button.
  • Or email hello@failosaurusrex.com and we will remove you within 48 hours.
  • Unsubscribing permanently deletes your email address and tokens from the subscriber database. We don't keep a "do not contact" copy, which means that if you sign up again later, you start fresh with a new confirmation email.

Plain English

Confirm to join, click once to leave, and when you leave your address is deleted rather than flagged.

We send email through our hosting provider's mail servers over an encrypted (STARTTLS) connection. We do not embed tracking pixels or link-tracking in our emails.

Cookies & local storage

This site does not set cookies for visitors, and its public pages do not use localStorage, sessionStorage or similar browser storage. We don't use analytics, advertising or social-media tracking scripts.

There is one exception. The private admin area, which only the operator can use, sets a single session cookie (named frx_admin) so the operator stays logged in. It is strictly functional, holds no tracking information, and exists only if someone opens the admin login page. Normal visitors who never visit that page never receive it. Because it is essential to running a login, it needs no consent banner, and that is why this site doesn't have one.

Third-party requests (Google Fonts) are covered in the next section. Our site doesn't set cookies through them, though Google's own handling of requests is governed by Google's policies.

Data retention

  • Subscriber record (email, timestamp, tokens, confirmed status): kept while you're subscribed. Deleted immediately when you unsubscribe via the link, and within 48 hours if you ask by email.
  • Unconfirmed sign-ups: these are not currently deleted automatically. We keep them only so the confirmation link can still work, and we'll remove any such address when you ask.
  • Sign-up attempt IPs: purged once older than one hour, as described above.
  • Broadcast log: kept until the operator deletes it. It contains no content of your emails, and your address appears in it only if a delivery to you failed.
  • Emails you send us: kept as long as useful for the conversation; deleted on request.
  • Backups: our hosting provider may keep routine backups for a limited time. Deleted data can persist in those until they are overwritten, and we won't restore deleted subscribers from them.
  • Hosting logs and Google Fonts requests: kept according to those providers' own policies.

The public archive and RSS feed contain published newsletter issues and are kept indefinitely, since they are the publication itself.

Data security

I take reasonable, proportionate steps to protect what I hold:

  • Database credentials and email passwords are stored in a configuration file kept outside the public web folder, not in the site's code.
  • The broadcast log also lives outside the public web folder.
  • The admin area requires a password (stored hashed, not in plain text), and the admin pages are excluded from search engines via robots.txt.
  • Confirmation and unsubscribe links use long random tokens that can't be guessed.
  • Database queries are parameterised, and email travels between this site and our provider's mail server over an encrypted connection.
  • The sign-up form is rate-limited, and the form never reveals whether an address is already on the list.

No system is perfectly secure, and I can't promise that nothing will ever go wrong. If a breach affecting your personal data occurs, I'll tell you and any required authorities as the law requires, and as quickly as I reasonably can.

Your rights

I apply the rights below to everyone, wherever you live, even where a particular law's thresholds might not strictly cover a one-person newsletter.

Everyone

  • Access: ask what we hold about you and get a copy.
  • Correction: have inaccurate data fixed.
  • Deletion: have your data erased.
  • Portability: get your data in a common, machine-readable format (for example CSV).
  • Objection and restriction: object to or ask us to pause processing based on our legitimate interests.
  • Withdraw consent: at any time. Unsubscribing does exactly this. Withdrawal doesn't affect anything done before it.

EEA and UK residents (GDPR / UK GDPR)

You have all of the rights above under Articles 15 to 21, plus the right to complain to your local data protection authority. In the UK that's the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO). I'd appreciate the chance to fix things first, but you're never required to come to me before complaining.

California residents (CCPA / CPRA)

You may ask to know what personal information we have collected, used and disclosed; to delete it; to correct it; and to opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of personal information. We do not sell or share personal information, as those terms are defined in California law, so there is nothing to opt out of. We won't discriminate against you for exercising a privacy right.

Texas residents (TDPSA)

You may confirm whether we process your personal data and access it, correct it, delete it, obtain a portable copy, and opt out of the sale of personal data, targeted advertising and profiling. We do none of those three, and we collect no sensitive personal data. If we decline a request and you disagree, you can appeal by replying to our decision, or by emailing us with "Appeal" in the subject line. We'll respond to the appeal in writing within 60 days. If you're still not satisfied, you can contact the Texas Attorney General.

How to exercise your rights

  1. Email hello@failosaurusrex.com from the address you subscribed with and say what you'd like (access, correction, deletion, etc.).
  2. If the request comes from a different address, we may ask you to confirm you control the subscribed one, for example by replying from it or clicking a link we send there. We do this to make sure we don't give your data to someone else.
  3. We'll reply within 30 days. If a request is unusually complex and we need more time, we'll tell you why and by when, within any limit the law allows.
  4. There's no charge, unless a request is clearly baseless or excessive, in which case we'll explain before doing anything.

You can also authorise someone else to make a request for you. We'll need proof of that authorisation. And if we can't fulfil a request, in whole or in part, we'll tell you why.

Plain English

Want out, a copy, or a fix? Email us. If you only want to stop the emails, the unsubscribe link is the fastest route and deletes your address instantly.

We don't track you, so there is no tracking for a "Do Not Track" or Global Privacy Control signal to switch off. We treat such signals as a reasonable request to keep doing that.

International transfers

I operate from the United States. Our hosting and email provider runs data centres in several regions, and Google operates globally, so your data may be stored or processed in countries other than your own, including the United States, whose data protection laws may differ from those where you live.

Where you are in the EEA, UK or Switzerland, transfers happen on the basis of your consent when you sign up, and with providers that publish their own transfer safeguards. If you aren't comfortable with that, please don't subscribe, or ask us to delete your data. We're happy to give you more detail on request.

Children

This site and newsletter are not directed at children. We don't knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 13 (or under 16 where your local law sets the age of digital consent higher). If you think a child has signed up, email us and we'll delete the address promptly.

AI training & scraping

Our robots.txt asks known AI crawlers to stay away, but it is a signal, not the limit of the rule. The binding terms are in the Terms of Service.
